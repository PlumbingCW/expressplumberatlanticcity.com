Overflow right now: what to do first

Do not flush again. Reach behind the toilet, find the small shutoff valve on the wall or floor and turn it clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float or flapper chain so the tank stops refilling. Then put towels around the base and keep children and pets out of the room.

If water is coming from the floor drain, other fixtures or the tub when you flush, stop using every drain in the home. That pattern points past the toilet to the line behind it, which our sewer line cleaning service handles. Once the water is stopped, we can plan a same-day appointment when available or take a late-night call if the bathroom is still flooding.

Leaks at the base

A toilet sits on a wax ring that seals it to the drain flange. When the ring fails, or the flange under it is cracked or sits too low, water seeps out on every flush. The floor around the toilet turns soft, and the stain may reach the ceiling below. This is a common cause of hidden damage in condo and apartment buildings.

The fix is to pull the toilet, inspect the flange, replace the wax ring or repair the flange, and set the toilet back level. If the subfloor has rotted, that needs to be addressed first. A small drip you cannot trace may need leak detection to confirm the toilet is the source.

Running tanks and phantom flushes

A toilet that refills by itself every few minutes is leaking water from the tank into the bowl. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is not sealing. A hissing sound with no flush means the fill valve is not shutting off.

Left alone, a running toilet can waste many gallons a day and push up your water bill. For rental properties with frequent turnover, we can check the flapper and fill valve at the same time as other fixtures. A dripping sink next door may need our faucet repair work in the same visit.

Clogs, and when the problem is deeper

A single clog is usually fixed with a plunger or a closet auger. If the toilet clogs repeatedly, gurgles when other fixtures run or backs up the tub, the blockage may sit in the main line. Plain drain problems fit our drain cleaning work, and main line problems are a sewer job.

Do not pour chemical cleaners into a stopped-up toilet. They sit in the bowl, can splash onto skin and make later work on the line less safe for the person doing it.

Cracked bowl or tank: repair or replace

A hairline crack in a tank can sometimes be managed for a short time, but a cracked bowl or a tank that leaks needs replacement. When we install a new toilet, we check the rough-in distance from the wall to the drain, the flange height and the condition of the floor first. Measuring beforehand avoids buying a toilet that does not fit.

Short-term rentals and busy bathrooms

Toilets in vacation rentals and shared buildings take more abuse than a single-family bath.
