Commercial plumbing emergency: what to do first

Protect people and property before you worry about the pipe. Stop using the affected fixtures, close the nearest shutoff, and keep staff and customers out of wet areas. If sewage is on the floor, treat it as contaminated and keep food and prep surfaces away from it.

  • Find the main shutoff and confirm everyone who works in the building knows where it is.
  • Switch off power to any equipment standing in water, but only if you can reach the breaker while dry.
  • Move stock, records and electronics up off the floor.
  • Note which drains, floors or units are affected and take photos for the work order.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call South Jersey Gas at 1-800-582-7060 or 911 before calling us.

Then call. We take the details, plan the visit, and offer same-day appointments when available. Late-night and weekend calls go to the same line, because a flooded kitchen or a burst riser does not keep office hours.

Floor drains, lift pumps and drain stoppages

Commercial drains carry more load than a house line. A floor drain that backs up usually points to a clogged branch or main, not a surface problem, and we locate the blockage before we choose the method. Heavy build-up may call for drain cleaning with a cable, or hydro-jetting to scour the pipe wall.

Where a building pumps its drainage up to the sewer, such as a below-grade restroom or a floor drain pit, a failed float switch or a stuck check valve makes the pit fill. We service the pump, the float and the valve, and we tell you plainly when a pump has reached the end of its life.

Grease trap cleaning for kitchens

A grease trap holds fats and food solids back from the sewer line. When it fills, the kitchen drains slow down and the smell gets worse, and the grease starts to harden in the pipe beyond it. We clean the trap, inspect its baffles and lid, and check the line it feeds.

How often a trap needs service depends on the menu, the fixture count and how much grease goes down the sink, so we do not quote a fixed schedule. Ask your local authority what applies to your kitchen, since rules for grease handling vary.

Backflow testing and assembly service

A backflow assembly keeps used water from creeping back into the drinking water supply. Some water utilities require annual testing of certain assemblies, and others do not. Ask your water utility whether your assembly needs annual testing, and tell us the make and location when you call.

This is planned work, not an emergency. We test and service assemblies, and we replace a worn assembly during a scheduled visit. A leaking relief valve or a cracked body deserves a prompt call, and a new assembly may involve plumbing installation work.

Storm drain and exterior drain cleaning

Parking-lot catch basins, yard drains and roof leaders fill with sand, leaves and trash. Atlantic City sits at about 7 feet in elevation, and water that cannot drain away during a heavy rain or a high tide pools at doorways. Clearing these drains before the forecast is cheaper than mopping a lobby after it.

We clean the catch basin, flush the line to the next structure, and report what we found. If the pipe is broken or crushed, we say so and recommend a camera look before any digging. Call 811 before you dig on any property.

Leak detection in a working building

A stain on a ceiling tile or a water meter that keeps moving when nothing runs both suggest a hidden leak. Our leak detection service uses acoustic and moisture tools to narrow the source before anyone opens a wall. Not every leak can be found without some opening, and we say so before the work.

What affects the cost of commercial plumbing work

Access to the pipe

A cleanout in a back hallway costs less labor than one behind equipment, in a ceiling or under a slab.

Size and type of line

A larger commercial drain or a grease line calls for heavier equipment than a single sink branch.

Scope of the problem

A clogged branch, a failed pump and a broken exterior line are three different jobs with different parts.

After-hours work

Timing can change the price of a call, and you hear the figure before work starts.

Permits and testing

Some work, such as assembly replacement, may need approval or a witnessed test. Ask the local construction office.

Commercial property and general liability policies often cover sudden water damage but not wear or maintenance. Ask your insurer or broker, and keep photos and the work order for any claim.
