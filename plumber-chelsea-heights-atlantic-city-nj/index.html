Chelsea Heights, Atlantic City

Plumber in Chelsea Heights, Atlantic City, NJ

Chelsea Heights is a named Atlantic City neighborhood with its own civic and taxpayers association. A federal and state study has looked at recurring flooding on its low-lying streets, so plumbing questions here often start with water that comes from outside the house. Flooding is the first plumbing question here

In 2016 the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and the New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection opened a cost-shared feasibility study of recurring flooding in Chelsea Heights. The Corps described the study area as about 0.2 square miles of low-lying residential streets bounded by North Albany Avenue, South Boulevard, North Raleigh Avenue and West End Avenue, with flooding that comes from tides combined with heavy rain and is growing more frequent.

A 2022 AFP report said Chelsea Heights streets still flood often. None of this says your house has a problem. It means the useful plumbing questions are about water coming in from outside, equipment sitting low, and what to do when the street is under water. Where the study stands today is a question for the city or the state.

Where Chelsea Heights is and where it is not

The city's neighborhood list includes Chelsea Heights, with the Chelsea Heights Civic and Taxpayers Association. Wikipedia places it west of Absecon Island and the Intracoastal Waterway, reached by the Route 40 bridge, and notes that it connects toward Ventnor Heights. Chelsea Heights School serves the neighborhood.

It is not the same as Chelsea, which has its own association and its own page. If your street is on the Ventnor side, the page for Ventnor City may fit better. A cross street on the phone sorts out which applies.

Housing: what is known and what is not

Real estate profile sites describe Chelsea Heights as mostly single-family homes with some small apartment buildings. Wikipedia says most homes were rebuilt over the past 30 to 35 years, but this page could not confirm that against an official source. Do not assume your own house's age or what is inside its walls. A plumber looking at the actual pipes is the only reliable answer.

Equipment height is a plumbing question

When a water heater is being replaced, it is fair to ask whether the unit can sit higher off the floor, how it will be vented and where the shutoff is. Water heater installation covers planned swaps, and the same questions apply to any appliance that lives on the lowest level.

If your street floods or a drain backs up, call (609) 547-1417 and say what the water is doing.

Local note

Gas, water and sewer: who to call first

If you smell gas after water has been in the house, leave on foot and call South Jersey Gas at 1-800-582-7060 or 911 before calling a plumber.

The water utility for Atlantic City is ACMUA, (609) 345-3315. Ask the city or ACMUA who owns the sewer main on your block before paying for work on a line that may not be yours.

  • Find the main water shutoff and the gas shutoff before the next forecast tide.
  • Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit to see whether the pump starts, if you have one.
  • Move stored items and cardboard off the floor of the lowest level.
  • Keep the phone numbers for the gas utility and ACMUA somewhere you can find them without power.
