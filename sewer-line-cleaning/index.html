Sewer backup: what to do first

  1. Stop using every drain and toilet in the building, and ask neighbors in a shared building to do the same.
  2. Keep people and pets away from any overflowing floor drain, tub or toilet, and wear gloves and boots if you must step in it.
  3. Move belongings, rugs and electronics off the floor, and turn off power to a flooded basement only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  4. Open windows for ventilation if the smell is strong, and call 911 if anyone feels dizzy or sick.
  5. Photograph the water line and affected items, then call us with the details.

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and the emergency line is staffed after hours. Calls at night get the same dispatch process as daytime calls, so describe which drains are backing up and how high the water is.

Finding the cleanout

A cleanout is a capped pipe, usually white plastic or black iron, that gives direct access to the main line. Look in the basement near the foundation wall, in a utility room, in a crawl space or outside along the foundation. Do not open it yourself if the line is full, since the standing sewage can pour out.

If you cannot find one, say so on the call. Without a cleanout, we may need to pull a toilet or work through a roof vent, which affects time and cost. A missing cleanout is also worth fixing later so future cleaning is simpler.

What we do on the main line

We run a heavy-duty cable with a cutting head through the cleanout to open the blockage, then flush the line with water to confirm flow. Where roots or grease have coated the walls, we use hydro jetting to clean the pipe rather than just opening a hole in it. If anything about the line looks wrong, we offer a camera look.

Cleaning is not repair. If the camera shows a broken or offset section, clearing it only buys time, and we tell you so. Our sewer line repair page explains what the next step involves.

Storms, high tides and backups

Atlantic City sits at about 7 feet in elevation on a barrier island, so tidal flooding is part of life here. The National Weather Service uses its Atlantic City tide gauge to define flood stages, at 6.0 feet for minor, 7.0 for moderate and 8.0 for major flooding. Sandy peaked at 8.8 feet on October 29, 2012. Reporting on the city also notes that water can rise through storm sewers during full-moon high tides and storms.

Here is what that means for you. If your backup happens during a storm or a very high tide, tell the dispatcher, because cleaning the line may not stop water entering from outside the pipe. We cannot tell you which streets flood, and we do not claim to. Ask the city or ACMUA about conditions on your street.

The Atlantic County Utilities Authority lists a wastewater treatment plant at 1801 Absecon Blvd in Atlantic City. Which agency owns the sewer main under your street is something to confirm with the city or ACMUA, and it matters if the blockage turns out to be beyond your property line.

After the water goes down

Treat any area touched by sewage as contaminated. Remove soaked porous items such as carpet padding and cardboard, wash hard surfaces with a household disinfectant and run fans to dry the space.

Photograph everything before you clean and keep damaged items until your insurer has seen them.

What affects the cost of sewer line cleaning

Cleanout access

A good cleanout speeds the job. Pulling a toilet or using a roof vent adds time.

Line length and depth

A longer run from the building to the main requires more cable and effort.

Cause of the stoppage

A soft grease plug is simpler than a mass of roots or a foreign object.

Method

Cable work and jetting use different equipment and time.

After-hours work
