Water heaters in a township that keeps adding homes

The Village at Farmington was approved for 667 homes, which is a reminder that new houses and older ones in the township ask different questions. In a new build, ask the builder what is covered before you call anyone. In an older house, a tank that is rusting at the seams or leaking at the base is a water heater replacement job, and we say so plainly instead of patching it.

Signs a tank is finished

  • Water pooling under the tank that does not come from a fitting or the relief valve.
  • Rust-colored hot water that clears when you run only the cold tap.
  • A rumbling or popping sound as sediment bakes onto the bottom of the tank.
  • Repeated repairs on the same unit within a short stretch.

If none of those fit and you simply have no hot water, a smaller part may have failed. We check before recommending a new tank. A leaking tank is an emergency because it can damage floors fast, so call the 24/7 line rather than waiting for morning.

When the whole house backs up, stop running water. Every flush and every load of laundry pushes more into a line that has nowhere to go. We clear the main with a cable or jetting, then run a camera if the blockage keeps coming back. Roots, grease and a belly in the pipe all look alike from the outside.

A sump pit is different. The pump has to start before water reaches the floor, so test it by pouring a bucket into the pit and watching the float rise. If it does not start, call us before the next storm. We do not assume every home has a pit or a basement, so tell us what your house has.

Before We Arrive: preparing a township home for the crew

Say which community you are in

Open a path

Note what changed
