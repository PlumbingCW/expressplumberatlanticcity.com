Rough-in and finish: the two visits behind most projects

Rough-in happens while the walls are open. We set drains, vents and supply lines at the right heights for the fixtures you picked, and we test the lines before they are covered. Finish comes later, when the fixtures, faucets and trim go on and every connection is tested again.

Giving us the fixture model numbers early prevents the most common delay, a drain or supply set a few inches off. If a remodeler is running the job, we coordinate with them on timing and leave the walls open until the inspection step your local rules require.

Permits and the construction office

New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code governs plumbing work, and the local construction official enforces it. Which jobs need a permit depends on the work and the town, so ask the construction office before you start. We do not guess at exemptions.

You can also check any plumber's license through the New Jersey State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. That is a sensible step for any installation, ours included. Before anyone digs outside for a new line, call 811 so the buried utilities are marked.

Water treatment: test your tap first

A softener or filter is planned work, and it is worth buying only against a problem you have confirmed. We do not assume a hardness level. The Atlantic City Municipal Utilities Authority water quality report does not list hardness, so use a home test kit or a lab test on your own tap.

If you are served by another utility, such as NJ American Water in Pleasantville, ask them what they report. Once you have results, we size a filter or softener to your plumbing, add a bypass and a drain connection, and explain the upkeep. Whole-home filtration and a point-of-use filter at the kitchen sink are different jobs, and we help you pick between them.

Gas-appliance hookups: safety before the wrench

Worth knowing

If you smell gas

Leave the building on foot, take everyone out, and avoid switches and phones near the leak. Call South Jersey Gas at 1-800-582-7060 or 911 from outside, before you call a plumber.

We connect appliances to an existing gas supply with the right shutoff and connector, and we test the joints. We do not promise a utility response time, and we will not hook up an appliance we think is unsafe.

New gas lines and meter work may involve the utility and the construction office, so ask before the appliance is delivered. A range, dryer or boiler swap is easier when the shutoff is already in place. Replacing a tank or tankless heater is covered under water heater installation.

Adding a bathroom or moving a fixture

The drain decides what is possible. Waste lines need a slope, a vent and a place to join the existing system. An addition in a basement or low room may need a sewage ejector pump, which we handle, and a below-grade bath adds planning time.

Before you commit, we look at the likely drain tie-in, the vent path and the water supply capacity. If the old supply pipes are corroded or too small for added fixtures, a repiping plan may belong in the budget. For a first look, expect a plain answer about what is easy and what is costly.

Shutoffs, backflow devices and the small parts that matter

  • Install a shutoff at every new fixture so the next repair does not mean closing the whole house.
  • Know where your main shutoff is and whether it turns easily, since a stuck valve is a bad surprise.
  • Ask your water utility whether any backflow device is required for your property, such as on a lawn irrigation line.
  • Add a pressure-relief or expansion tank where the setup calls for one.
  • Label the valves you add so the next person can find them.

If you already have a fixture that drips or runs, the fix is a repair, not an installation. Our faucet repair and toilet repair pages cover those, and urgent failures such as a leaking fixture are handled there at any hour. We also serve Pleasantville and nearby towns.

What affects the cost of plumbing installation

Number and type of fixtures

A kitchen with several fixtures costs more than one toilet swap, and premium fixtures add install time.

Access to walls and floors

Open walls during a remodel are easier than cutting into finished surfaces.

Pipe material and size

Copper, PEX and plastic drains differ in cost, and a larger line needs more material.

Length of new runs

A fixture moved a few feet costs less than one moved across the house.

Permits and inspections

Local fees and inspection scheduling affect the project timeline. Ask the construction office.

Homeowners policies generally do not pay for upgrades or new installations, and they cover only sudden and accidental damage. If a leak comes from new work, tell your insurer early. Ask your insurer what applies.
