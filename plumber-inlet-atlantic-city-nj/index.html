The Inlet, Atlantic City

Plumber in the Inlet, Atlantic City, NJ

The Inlet is a named Atlantic City neighborhood at the northern end of the city, near the Absecon Lighthouse. New townhomes are being built on lots that sat vacant for years, so plumbing questions here often start with a builder's warranty. A neighborhood where much of the housing is new

The Penn planning report says rowhouses were built in the South Inlet from roughly 1900 to 1952, and that much of it was cleared in the 1960s and 1970s after the area was declared blighted in 1964. Recent building has begun to fill some of those lots. Jersey Digs reported in January 2024 that Lighthouse Row, eight homes in four detached townhome buildings at 12 South New Hampshire Avenue, broke ground in late December 2023.

A builder's press release says K. Hovnanian Homes broke ground on August 12, 2026 on 38 waterfront townhomes near Gardner's Basin, across from Altman Park. This page does not know when any of these homes will be occupied. It does mean the Inlet has less old plumbing in the picture than the rowhouse blocks of Ducktown or Chelsea, which is why it focuses on warranties and hookups.

Where the Inlet is

The city's neighborhood list includes the Inlet, with the 1st Ward Civic Association. Wikipedia places South Inlet in the northern part of the city, overlooking Absecon Inlet, where the Absecon Lighthouse stands. The Penn report bounds it by the Boardwalk to the south and east and Gramercy Avenue to the north, with an irregular western edge between Metropolitan and Connecticut Avenues. Lighthouse Row sits between Atlantic and Pacific Avenues.

Hurricane Sandy destroyed the northern Boardwalk in the South Inlet residential section in 2012. That is a reason to know where your shutoff valves are and to ask about raising equipment off the floor, not a claim about any one home. The 2018 mayor's comment on flood gates referred to reducing flooding from Ducktown to the Inlet.

Worth knowing

Warranty first, then repairs

In a newly built home, the builder's warranty may cover a fault that would otherwise cost you a repair. Read it before you call, and report problems in writing so there is a date on record. A plumber can still tell you what is wrong while you sort out who pays.

Local note

Gas, water and sewer: who to call first

If you smell gas, leave on foot and call South Jersey Gas at 1-800-582-7060 or 911 before calling a plumber.

For a water utility question, ACMUA serves Atlantic City at (609) 345-3315. Ask the city or ACMUA who owns the sewer main on your street, since it should be confirmed street by street.

Before you call about a plumbing problem in the Inlet

  1. 1

    Close the nearest shutoff

    Turn the fixture valve off first. If that does not stop the water, close the main shutoff.

  2. 2

    Find your paperwork

    A new home may have a warranty, a closing folder and appliance manuals that answer the question before a plumber is needed.

  3. 3

    Photograph the problem

    A picture of a stain, a drip or an error code on a heater display is quicker than describing it.

  4. 4

    Give a cross street

    New streets and renamed lots are easy to confuse on a map. A cross street fixes the address.
