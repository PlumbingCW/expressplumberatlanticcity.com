Leak emergency: a valve that will not shut off

The worst version of a faucet problem is a handle that spins freely or a shutoff valve that will not close. Water keeps running and you cannot isolate the fixture. Go to the main shutoff, usually where the line enters the building or by the meter, and turn it clockwise until the flow stops.

Then call. Do not force an old angle stop with a wrench, since the stem can snap and open the line. If the main will not close either, tell us right away, because that is a bigger emergency. Our crews take these calls overnight and on weekends, and a less urgent drip can wait for a same-day appointment when available.

Find your shutoffs before you need them

  • Sinks and toilets have small oval or round valves on the wall or floor below the fixture, called angle stops.
  • Tubs and showers usually have no stop at the fixture, so the shutoff is the main valve or a separate zone valve.
  • Outdoor spigots often have an interior shutoff just inside the wall, in a basement or crawlspace.
  • The water heater has its own cold inlet valve on top of the tank.
  • The main shutoff sits near the meter or where the line enters the building.

Turn each valve once a year so it does not seize. A stiff or corroded stop is a sign it should be replaced during the next repair, not at 2 a.m. during a leak.

A faucet is cheap to repair compared with the water it wastes, and a steady drip can add up on a bill. We find the exact model, match parts and test the fix. If the faucet is discontinued or heavily corroded, we tell you plainly when replacement makes more sense than another repair.

Showers, tubs and hidden valves

A shower that drips from the head, runs lukewarm or will not shut off is usually a worn cartridge in the shower valve, not the showerhead. A tub spout that leaks from behind the wall can mean a failed diverter or a pipe joint inside. These are often fixed through the trim opening, without cutting tile.

If water stains appear on the ceiling below a bathroom or the wall behind the tub feels damp, the problem may be larger than a worn part. Our leak detection service traces the source before anything is opened. A toilet running in the same bathroom may be handled with toilet repair on the same visit.

Kitchen sink, disposal and supply lines

Look under the sink when a drip appears. The braided supply lines, the stops and the trap are all common sources, and each can look like a faucet leak from above. A wet cabinet floor that dries and returns is a typical sign. We check the whole sink set before replacing the faucet, so you do not pay for the wrong part.

A disposal that leaks at the flange or housing is a separate job covered by garbage disposal repair. If we find it during a faucet visit, we show you where the water is coming from and quote the work in advance.

Outdoor spigots in cold weather

A hose left attached through winter can trap water in the spigot and freeze it, splitting the pipe inside the wall. The leak often shows only when the weather warms, as water inside the wall or crawlspace. Disconnect hoses in fall, shut the interior valve if there is one and open the outdoor spigot to drain it. Atlantic City sees snowfall of 12 to 18 inches between November and April, so freezing is a real concern.
