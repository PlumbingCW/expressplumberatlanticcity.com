Ducktown, Atlantic City

Plumber in Ducktown, Atlantic City, NJ

Ducktown is a long-established residential neighborhood in Atlantic City with its own revitalization association. Much of it is rowhouses and small buildings with apartments in the rear or the basement, so the first plumbing question is often who is responsible for the pipe. Rowhouses, rear apartments and who calls the plumber

A University of Pennsylvania planning report on Ducktown describes two- and three-story detached and semi-detached rowhouses, plus alleys of small rowhouses such as Italy Terrace. It says many rowhouses had rentable apartments at the rear or finished basements. It also says a sizeable share of the buildings predate 1939, that post-2000 construction is under 10 percent, and that the area is largely renter-occupied.

That points to a simple first step. In a building with more than one household, who calls the plumber depends on who owns the building and what the lease says. Tenants usually start with the landlord or manager. Owners of a whole rowhouse can call directly. Building age tells you nothing certain about the pipes, so describe the symptom instead of guessing at the cause.

Where Ducktown is on the map

The city's neighborhood list includes Ducktown, with the Ducktown Revitalization Association. Sources describe the edges differently. Wikipedia has it stretching from Missouri Avenue to Texas Avenue. The Penn report's residential boundary runs from Missouri to California Avenues and from Pacific Avenue to the bay, and the early-1900s neighborhood was wider, from Arkansas to California Avenue.

Boardwalk Hall sits on Mississippi Avenue in the neighborhood, and the Atlantic City rail terminal is about a block away. South of Texas Avenue is Chelsea, a separate neighborhood. If boundaries are unclear for your block, a cross street over the phone is enough.

Storm drains and the Baltic Avenue Canal

Atlantic City's Baltic Avenue canal dates from 1912 and connects to hundreds of storm drains. A rebuild reported at 22 million dollars began in 2024 with new pumps planned at Atlantis Avenue. When a flood gate project was completed in 2018, the mayor said it should help reduce flooding from the Ducktown area to the Inlet. The two projects are about street water, not about the pipes in your house.

The tie-back for a homeowner is practical. If water backs up through drains during heavy rain or a high tide, the cause may be in the street, and the city is the first call. If water is coming from a fixture or a pipe inside, that is plumbing. Sewer line cleaning covers backups that start in the building's own line.

Local note

Gas, water and sewer: who to call first

If you smell gas, leave on foot and call South Jersey Gas at 1-800-582-7060 or 911 before you call a plumber.

For a street-side water problem, call ACMUA at (609) 345-3315. Ask the city or ACMUA who owns the sewer main on your block, because it should be confirmed street by street.

Ducktown plumbing questions to ask before work starts

Which unit is affected?

In a multi-unit building, naming the unit and the floor keeps the plumber from checking the wrong line.

Is the line shared?

A backup that reaches two units often points to a line they share. A backup in one unit may be local to it.

Who approves the work?

Landlords and owners decide who repairs what. Settle that before a plumber opens a wall.

Is a permit needed?

Rules vary by job. Ask the local construction office before planned work such as fixture changes or a new water heater.
