The first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

  1. 1

    Stop the water

    Close the fixture's angle stop if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If water is coming from a wall, ceiling or pipe, close the main shutoff where the line enters the building and open a faucet low in the house to drain the line. If you can reach it dry, shut off power or gas to the water heater as well.

  2. 2

    Protect people and electricity

    Stay out of standing water near outlets, panels and appliances. If water is reaching electrical equipment, leave the area and call 911 first. Smell gas? Leave on foot and call South Jersey Gas at 1-800-582-7060 or 911 before you call a plumber.

  3. 3

    Stop using the drains

    When a toilet gurgles or a tub fills as the washer runs, the main line is full. Every flush or load adds to the overflow, so hold off until we have looked at it.

  4. 4

    Call the 24/7 line

    Dial (609) 547-1417 and tell dispatch the address, what you see and what you have already shut off. That is enough for us to line up the right tools and book the earliest slot.

How tides and storms shape plumbing calls in Atlantic City

Atlantic City sits at about 7 feet above sea level on Absecon Island. The National Weather Service rates the Atlantic City tide gauge at 6 feet for minor tidal flooding, 7 feet for moderate flooding with widespread roadway water and 8 feet for major flooding. Hurricane Sandy peaked at 8.8 feet on October 29, 2012.

For a homeowner, the lesson is practical. When high water sits against the storm drains, a sewer line that works fine at low tide can back up at the next high one. If your drains gurgle or slow down only around high tide, tell dispatch, because it helps us decide whether to start with your own sewer line cleaning or to point you to the street-side agency.

Rutgers researchers project 17 to 75 days a year of high-tide flooding by 2030 in Atlantic City, compared with less than once a year in 1950. Low rooms, crawlspaces and below-grade bathrooms are where that water shows up, which is why a working sump or ejector pump and a clear main line are worth checking before the next storm.

Worth knowing

Who to call: the utility or the plumber

Water bubbling up in the street or a dead-flat loss of pressure on your whole block is a utility problem. The Atlantic City Municipal Utilities Authority can be reached at (609) 345-3315, and New Jersey American Water customers in towns such as Pleasantville and Absecon can call 1-800-272-1325.

Anything on your side of the meter, such as a leaking line to the house, a failed fixture or a clog, is a plumbing job, and that is where we come in. When you are unsure which side it is on, call us and we will tell you what to ask the utility.
