Start with the symptom, then find the cause

Most hidden leaks announce themselves long before anything drips. A water bill that jumped, a meter dial that creeps with every tap closed, a brown ring on a ceiling, a floor that feels warm underfoot, or a faint hiss in a quiet room all point to water escaping somewhere it should not. Leak detection is the work of tracing that symptom back to one pipe, one joint or one fitting.

The goal is to open as little of your home as possible. We confirm the leak first, narrow it to a few feet, and only then talk about repair. If the source turns out to be a split line, our burst pipe repair crews can handle the fix on the same visit when that is practical.

Meter still running with everything off

This is the cleanest test you can run yourself, and it settles the first big question: is the leak inside the house or out in the yard? Do it before you call so you can tell us what you saw.

  1. Turn off every faucet, the dishwasher, the washing machine and the ice maker.
  2. Find your meter, usually in a box near the curb or inside at the point where the line enters.
  3. Lift the lid, wipe the glass and look for the small leak indicator or the sweep hand. Any movement means water is passing.
  4. Close your main shutoff valve at the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps turning, the leak is on the pipe between the meter and the house.
  5. Write down the reading and the time, wait an hour, and read it again.

For account and meter questions in Atlantic City, ACMUA can be reached at (609) 345-3315. Customers in the New Jersey American Water towns nearby can call 1-800-272-1325. Either utility can confirm your usage history, which helps us see when the leak began.

Stains, damp walls and warm floors

Ceiling and wall leaks are the most misleading. Water travels along joists and pipes, so the stain is rarely under the source. A spot in the bedroom may come from a bathroom supply line two feet away, and a stain that only appears after heavy rain is a roof or window problem, not a plumbing one. We tell you which it is, even when the answer means you need a roofer instead of us.

A warm patch on a floor points to a hot water line running under or inside the slab. Slab leaks do not fix themselves, and they can run for months. Because the line is buried in concrete, finding the exact spot matters more here than anywhere else, since it decides whether you need a small access cut or a reroute of that line.

How a leak gets located without guessing

No single tool finds every leak. We pick methods based on what the symptom suggests and combine them when the first result is unclear.

Crawlspaces, slabs and buried lines

Atlantic City sits at about 7 feet of elevation on a barrier island, so lines often run through wet ground. A leak under a crawlspace often shows up as damp insulation, a musty smell or sagging subfloor. Underground, a leaking service line can soften soil or drop the pressure at every tap. If we trace the water to the pipe between the street and your building, the next step is covered on our water service line repair page.

Outdoor leaks get their own check. Irrigation zones, a pool or spa fill line and a cracked meter box can each look like a house leak on the bill. We run each system separately so you pay to fix the right one. Call 811 before any digging so buried utilities are marked, and we will never dig without that step.

When a hidden leak becomes an emergency

Call right away if water is coming through a ceiling, if a stain is spreading by the hour, if you hear running water near electrical panels, or if the floor is soft. Shut off the main, turn off power to affected rooms if you can reach the panel safely, move belongings out of the wet area and take photos for your insurer. If you smell gas, leave on foot and call South Jersey Gas at 1-800-582-7060 or 911 first.

We take after-hours leak calls from Atlantic City and nearby towns such as Ventnor City any night of the week, and we schedule same-day appointments when available for leaks that are slow but still costing you.
