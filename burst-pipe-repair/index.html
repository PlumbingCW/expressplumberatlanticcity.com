The first five minutes matter most

What you do before a plumber reaches you decides how much of the house gets wet. Work through this list in order and stop at any step that feels unsafe.

  1. Close the main water shutoff, usually near the meter, where the line enters the basement or in a utility closet. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain what is left in the pipes.
  3. If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, switch off power at the breaker only if you can reach it while standing on dry floor. Otherwise stay out and call.
  4. Move rugs, electronics and paper up and away from the wet area.
  5. Photograph the damage and the pipe before anything is touched. Your insurer will want them.

If you cannot find or turn the main valve, tell us when you call. A stuck valve is a repair of its own, and we can talk you through other ways to slow the water. If you suspect gas near the wet area, leave on foot and call South Jersey Gas at 1-800-582-7060 or 911 before you phone a plumber.

When a burst pipe is an emergency

If water is flowing and you cannot stop it, treat it as an emergency. The same goes for a pipe that has split inside a wall, a ceiling that is bulging with water, or any leak that continues after the main is closed. A weeping joint that you can contain with a bucket is urgent but not a flood, and we can often handle it with a same-day appointment when available.

After hours, the call goes to our dispatch line and the job is triaged by how much water is moving. We do not promise arrival times, but we do tell you honestly what we can do tonight and what can wait until morning. A related symptom with no visible burst may be a slow hidden leak, which is what our leak detection service is for.

What the repair looks like

A plumber starts by isolating the damaged section so the rest of the house keeps water where possible. The failed length is cut out, the ends are cleaned and a new section is joined in with fittings suited to the pipe material. Then the line is pressurized and watched for weeping before anything is closed up.

If the pipe is old and brittle in more than one place, a spot repair may only move the leak to the next weak joint. We tell you plainly when a run is worn out, and explain what repiping would involve compared with fixing one section.

Frozen pipes in an Atlantic City winter

The airport has recorded a low of minus 11 F, and winter snowfall here typically totals 12 to 18 inches between November and April. A cold snap does not need to be that extreme to split a pipe in an unheated crawlspace, an exterior wall or a vacant unit. Water expands as it freezes, and the split often opens only once the ice melts, so the flood can start after the weather warms up.

If a pipe is frozen but has not burst, open the faucet slightly, warm the area with a hair dryer on low or a space heater placed well away from anything flammable, and never use a flame. If you see frost on the pipe or no water at one tap, treat the pipe as already damaged until it is checked. Seasonal rentals and vacant homes are the most exposed because nobody notices until the damage is done.

Cast iron and older drain lines

Burst water lines get the attention, but old cast iron drains crack too, and the result is a different kind of mess. A cracked drain leaks only when it carries water, so stains show up after laundry or a shower. We repair a cracked section by cutting it out and fitting a new piece, or we recommend replacement when the pipe is scaled and thin along its length.
