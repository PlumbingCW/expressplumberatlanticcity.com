When the tide and wind decide your plumbing week

Ventnor City sits on Absecon Island next to Atlantic City, and close to half of its 3.53 square miles is water. The nearest tide gauge we can cite is the Atlantic City gauge, which the National Weather Service reads in feet above mean lower low water. It gives you a plain way to judge when to check your own building.

Atlantic City tide gauge readings and what they can mean for a Ventnor building
Tide readingNational Weather Service labelWhat to check at home

Sandy's peak at the Atlantic City gauge was 8.8 ft on Oct 29, 2012, so treat that as a reference for the worst recent surge, not a forecast for your block. Back-bay water can keep rising over several tides when an onshore wind blocks drainage, so one calm high tide does not mean the danger has passed. Water can also come up through storm sewers during full-moon high tides and storms.

After any flood, do not run a heater, washer or furnace that sat in water until it has been checked. The Army Corps beach and dune system along Absecon Island works on the ocean side, and it does not change what happens inside your pipes, so the checks above still apply.

If water has reached a heater, boiler or panel, call us on the 24/7 line and stay out of the room. Electricity and standing water do not mix, and we will not ask you to take a risk to reach a valve.

Ventnor runs its own water and sewer department, so you deal with a city office rather than a large company. The office at 111 N Cornwall Ave. is open weekdays 8:30 to 4:30, and its number is 609-823-7935. The city draws from six wells in the Kirkwood aquifer, about 800 feet down, and adds chlorine with gas. We do not quote hardness, so ask the office and test your tap.

Wastewater travels through city pump stations to a county pump station and then to a regional plant. That chain is why a block-wide backup is worth a call to the city. We clear and repair what is on your side. Call the water and sewer department at 609-823-7935 and ask which section of line is theirs, because we do not guess ownership.

Check the meter first when a home has sat empty

Any home left empty for a few weeks can leak unnoticed, so check the meter and a few faucets on return. A high bill, a stained ceiling or the sound of running water are the other signs. Read the meter with everything off, and if it moves, leak detection finds the line instead of cutting walls on a hunch.

If you plan to leave the house empty for weeks, shut the main when you go and open a tap or two to relieve pressure. Ask the city water office where your meter box is before the first time you do it. A shutoff you cannot find at midnight is the same as no shutoff.

Hot water and drains on the island

A rusting tank, a weeping fitting or a pilot that will not stay lit deserves a look before it fails completely. Water heater repair covers failed elements, valves and igniters. If the shell leaks, we tell you that the tank is done and price a swap separately.

Slow drains behave differently when the building has been empty for weeks. A trap can dry out and let odor in, and grease can set in a line that nobody used. Drain cleaning handles the sink, tub and floor drain. For a main-line stoppage, we run a camera so you can see what is in the line.

Paperwork, permits and your insurer

We are licensed and insured, and you can check any plumber through the NJ State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. Before a heater swap or a line replacement, ask the Ventnor construction office whether the job needs a permit, since New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code applies to plumbing work and rules vary by job. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. Call 811 before any digging.

Insurers usually look at sudden and accidental water damage and not at wear or maintenance. Tide water from outside is often a separate flood question, so ask your insurer which policy applies before repairs begin.

Boardwalk side or bay side: what to tell dispatch

The Ventnor Boardwalk runs 1.7 miles along the beach, joining Atlantic City's Boardwalk and ending at Margate City, so the island has a clear ocean edge and a clear bay edge. When you call, give us the nearest cross street and say if you are Boardwalk side or bay side. That lets us confirm the address and ask the right questions before anyone drives out.

Margate City and Longport are neighboring towns on the island. If you also own a place on the mainland, our pages for Pleasantville and Egg Harbor Township cover those addresses.
