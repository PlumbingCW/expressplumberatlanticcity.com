Who is responsible for which part of the line

The service line is the pipe that carries water from the main under the street, through the meter, and into your building. People often assume it is all the utility's problem or all theirs. In practice the split usually falls at the meter, with the utility responsible up to it and the owner responsible after it. Because that point can differ by utility and by property, ask your water utility where its responsibility ends before you pay for any digging.

Signs the line is leaking underground

  • A wet, soft or sunken patch of ground between the curb and the house, with no rain to explain it.
  • A meter that keeps turning after every fixture in the house is shut off.
  • A water bill that climbs with no change in how you use water.
  • The sound of running water near the entry point when the house is quiet.
  • Muddy or discolored water, or a sudden drop in pressure at every tap.

None of these proves a service line leak on its own. A running toilet or a leaking supply inside the building can look the same on a meter, which is why we separate the two with a quick shutoff test. If the problem is inside, our leak detection work finds it. If the meter keeps spinning with the main closed, the underground line is the suspect.

When a service line failure is an emergency

Water bubbling up in the yard, a sinkhole forming near the foundation, no water at all, or a basement taking on water from outside are emergencies. Close the main valve at the house, stay clear of flooded areas near electrical equipment, and call. We take emergency calls at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are offered when available for slower leaks.

If the water is clean and flowing from the ground, do not try to patch it yourself. A cracked line under pressure can undermine a driveway or slab within hours. We locate the break, tell you where the utility's part ends, and give a clear price before work starts.

Low water pressure: check the simple things first

Weak pressure does not always mean a leak. Work through the likely causes before assuming the line is damaged.

  1. Test pressure at an outdoor spigot and an indoor tap. If both are weak, the issue is upstream of the fixtures.
  2. Check that the main shutoff valve is fully open. A valve left a quarter turn closed after past work is a common culprit.
  3. Look for a pressure reducing valve near the entry. A failing one can choke flow or let pressure spike.
  4. Ask a neighbor or the utility if there is work on the main.
  5. Consider the pipe itself. Old galvanized service lines scale on the inside and shrink the opening.

If the line is the cause, the choice is between a spot repair and replacement. Where the pipe is narrowed along its whole length, a full replacement is usually the sensible route, and our repiping team can also tie the new line into the interior piping.

What affects the cost of service line repair

Length and depth

A short, shallow line costs less than a long, deep one.

Pipe material

Copper, plastic and lead-free options differ in cost and labor.

Surface above the line

Lawn, concrete and asphalt each change restoration work.

Method

Open trench and trenchless approaches use different equipment.

Permits and utility coordination

Local rules and shutoff scheduling can affect timing.
