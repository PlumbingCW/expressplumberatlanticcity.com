Disposal leaking under the sink: what to do first

Water under the cabinet is the call we hear most. Turn the wall switch off, then unplug the unit or flip its breaker, and set a bowl or baking tray under the leak. Do not run the dishwasher or the faucet again until someone has looked at it.

  • Empty the cabinet floor of cleaners and boxes so the wood stays dry.
  • Wipe the underside of the unit and the pipes with a dry towel, then watch where the first drop forms.
  • Take a phone photo of the drip point and of the label on the side of the unit.
  • If water is reaching an outlet or the cord, shut off the kitchen breaker before you touch anything.

The photo and the label help us bring the right seal kit or a matching replacement. If the sink basin itself is overflowing rather than the unit leaking, the problem is likely a clogged line, which our drain cleaning service handles.

Jam or hum: the reset button and the hex key

A hum with no spinning means the motor is trying to turn a stuck plate. Cut power first, at the switch and at the plug or breaker. Then look under the unit for a small hex socket in the center and a red reset button near the bottom edge.

Working the hex key back and forth frees most light jams. Press reset after the plate moves freely, then restore power and run cold water. Never put a hand, a spoon or tongs into the chamber to dig, even with the power off, because the plate can move and the blades are sharp. If the unit trips again or the hum returns, stop and call.

Where the leak starts: flange, body or drain connection

Three leak points and what each one costs you in repair scope

Flange at the sink drain

Water at the top rim means the seal or plumber's putty under the sink flange has failed. This is often a reseat and not a new unit.

Housing body

A leak from the side or bottom of the shell usually means internal seals or the casing have given out. Replacement is the sensible path here.

Drain and trap connections

Loose slip nuts, a cracked discharge elbow or a worn gasket leak when the unit runs. These repairs are small and the disposal keeps its life.

Dishwasher hose

A clamp that has loosened on the dishwasher inlet drips only during a dishwasher cycle. We re-seat the line and test it under load.

Repair or replace: how we decide

We open the cabinet, find the exact leak or fault, and tell you the price before work starts. A jam, a tripped reset or a loose connection is a repair. A cracked body, a seized motor or a unit that has leaked from the shell is a replacement.

Most households choose between a modest unit and a heavier one with more motor power. Heavier units cost more and grind more, but they only help if your drain line can carry the extra flow away. If the line is the weak point, a replacement will not solve the slow drain.

Power, sizing and the dishwasher connection

A disposal either plugs into an outlet under the sink or is hardwired to a switch. We match what is already there. If the outlet or wiring looks wrong, we tell you and recommend a licensed electrician for that part, because we only do plumbing.

The dishwasher drain ties into the disposal inlet through a knockout plug that must be removed on a new unit, or the dishwasher will not drain. We also check the high loop or air gap on the dishwasher line, which keeps dirty water from flowing back. New fixtures nearby may call for plumbing installation work, and a dripping faucet above the sink is a separate faucet repair.

What should never go down the disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat, which coat the drain line and harden into a plug further down.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in quantity, since they pack into sludge in the trap.
  • Fibrous scraps such as celery, corn husks and onion skins.
  • Rice, pasta and potato peels, which swell and turn to paste.
  • Bones, fruit pits and anything hard, plus glass, metal or plastic.

If the drain behind the disposal is already slow, grinding more food only makes it worse. Repeat clogs in a kitchen line sometimes need hydro-jetting to scour the pipe clean, which we explain at the visit.

What affects the cost of garbage disposal repair

Repair or replacement

A freed jam or reseated flange costs far less than a new unit with installation.

Unit size and type

Heavier motors and sound-insulated models cost more than basic ones.

Wiring and plug type

A hardwired unit or a missing outlet adds work that a plug-in swap avoids.

Condition of the drain below

Corroded slip nuts, a cracked elbow or a clogged line can add parts or a drain service.
