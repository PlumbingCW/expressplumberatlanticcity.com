How a tankless unit heats water

When you open a hot tap, a flow sensor tells the unit to fire. Gas burners or electric elements heat the water as it passes through a heat exchanger, and the heater shuts off when the tap closes. There is no stored supply, so the limit is how much water the unit can heat at once, not how much it holds.

That limit is called the flow rate rise. A unit that handles one shower may struggle with two showers and a dishwasher at the same time. We size to your busiest moment, not your average day. If the heater has stopped working altogether, call about water heater repair for a fast diagnosis.

Gas line, vent and install requirements

A gas tankless unit burns a lot of fuel quickly, so the gas pipe often has to be larger than the one that served a tank. The meter, regulator and pipe run all matter. A licensed plumber measures the load and tells you whether a larger line is needed. South Jersey Gas handles the meter and utility side.

The vent is just as important. Condensing models use plastic pipe that exits through a side wall. We check termination distances from windows and doors, and the run length. Electric tankless units skip the vent but need heavy electrical service, which we check against your panel.

  • Gas line capacity for the unit's full burner load.
  • Vent route, material and exit location.
  • Condensate drain for condensing models.
  • Electrical outlet for the fan and controls, or a heavy circuit for electric models.
  • Water pressure and flow at the fixtures you use together.
  • Shutoff and service valves so the unit can be flushed.

Flushing and descaling: why and when

Minerals in water can build up inside the heat exchanger. That slows heat transfer, raises temperatures inside the unit, and can trigger error codes. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit and then rinses it out. It needs service valves, so we install those on every new unit.

How often to flush depends on your water, so we do not set a number for you. Atlantic City Municipal Utilities Authority posts its water quality report, but it does not give a hardness figure, so test your tap or ask your utility if you want to know. Signs it is time include longer waits for hot water and changing temperature.

Error symptoms and the cold water sandwich

If the unit shows an error code, write it down, because the code points to ignition, exhaust, sensor or flow trouble. Many codes clear after a power reset and a flush. A cold burst between two hot ones is called the cold water sandwich. It happens when you shut the tap briefly and reopen it, and the water in the pipes cools.

Winter, freezing and where the unit goes

The airport has recorded a low of minus 11 F, and winter snowfall typically runs 12 to 18 inches. An indoor unit is shielded from that. An exterior unit relies on built-in freeze protection that needs power to work, so a long outage can matter. We talk through the location choice with you and avoid placing a unit where it cannot be serviced.

What affects the cost of a tankless water heater

Unit capacity

Higher flow models cost more than small ones.

Gas line upsizing

A longer or larger gas pipe adds materials and labor.

Venting

Side wall venting and condensate drains vary with the layout.

Electrical needs

Fan power, controls or heavy circuits change the scope.

Service valves and flushing

Valves, flush time and parts for scale or sensor trouble vary by unit.

Scale and age-related failures are maintenance issues that policies generally exclude. Ask your insurer what applies.
