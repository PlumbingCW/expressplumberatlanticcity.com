When a clogged drain is an emergency

A clog turns into an emergency when the water has nowhere left to go. Standing water that keeps rising after the tap is off, a toilet that bubbles when the shower drains, or a sewage smell with wet floors all fit. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and the 24/7 emergency line is the number to use at night or on a weekend.

  • Stop using every fixture on the affected line, including the dishwasher and the washing machine.
  • Lay towels at the base of a tub or floor drain that is rising, and lift rugs out of the way.
  • Skip chemical drain cleaner on standing water. It sits in the pipe and can splash onto skin when the line is opened.
  • Take photos of any water on the floor if you may contact your insurer later.
  • If sewage is coming up, keep children and pets out of the room and wear gloves while you mop.

One drain or several: how we read the clog

The first question on the phone is how many fixtures are slow. One slow drain points to the trap or a short branch near that fixture. Several slow drains at once point to a shared branch or the main line, and the tools and the price change with that answer.

Kitchen sinks: grease, food and the disposal

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Cooking fat runs through the pipe as a liquid, cools on the pipe wall and traps rice, coffee grounds and peelings until the opening closes. Hot tap water moves it a few feet down the line, where it sets again. That is why a kitchen sink can drain fine for a day after a home fix and then stop.

Scrape plates into the trash, wipe greasy pans with a paper towel and keep fibrous scraps out of the sink. If your sink has a disposal and the drain is clogged beyond it, the unit may be part of the problem, so see our garbage disposal repair page. A business with a busy kitchen, such as a Boardwalk food stand, deals with the same grease in larger amounts and should ask about cleaning on a set schedule.

Tubs, showers and the leak you might not see

Shower and tub clogs are mostly hair wound around the stopper or the first bend, bound together with soap film. A drain strainer that you empty weekly prevents most of them. When we open a tub drain, we pull the hair out by hand or with a cable rather than pushing it deeper into the line.

A drain line leak is a separate problem, and cleaning will not fix it. Signs are a stain on the ceiling below a bathroom, a damp cabinet, a musty smell or water that appears only when the drain is in use. Mention any of these on the call so the plumber checks the joints and traps as well as the clog.

Cable, jetting or camera: choosing the tool

A cable, sometimes called a snake, turns a cutting head through the blockage and is the right first move for most single-fixture clogs. It opens a hole through the clog, but it can leave a coating of grease on the pipe wall. When a line was cabled and clogged again, hydro jetting scours the wall with high-pressure water and is often the better fit.

If a clog keeps returning with no obvious cause, a camera shows what the cable cannot feel. A collapsed section, an offset joint or a root mass looks very different on screen than a grease buildup. We discuss a full camera sewer inspection when the evidence points that way, and we say so before adding it to the job.

What affects the cost of drain cleaning

Which line is blocked

A bathroom sink trap is quick work. A branch line behind a wall or the main line takes more equipment and more time.

Access

A clear cleanout makes the job simpler. Without one, we may need to pull a toilet or work from a roof vent.

Pipe material and condition

Old cast iron with a rough interior holds debris and may need a gentler approach than newer plastic pipe.

Tool required

Hand auger, cable machine, jetter and camera each carry different equipment time.

Timing of the visit

The time of day or week can affect the price. Whatever the hour, you hear the figure before work starts.

Apartments, condos and rowhouses

Atlantic City has many apartments, condominiums and rowhouses, and in those buildings several units can share one drain stack. If your clog is in a shared line, the building owner or association may be the one who arranges and pays for the work. Check with your property manager before you call, or call us and we will tell you what we see so you can pass it along.

In a rowhouse or an older multi-family building, a slow ground-floor drain may be a main line issue even when the upstairs drains are fine, so note which floors are affected before you call. The same shared-stack question applies in Ventnor City, which we also serve.
