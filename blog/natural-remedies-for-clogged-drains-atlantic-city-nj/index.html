Natural Remedies for Clogged Drains in Atlantic City, NJ

A plunger and a hand snake clear most single-fixture clogs, boiling water helps on grease in metal pipes, and baking soda with vinegar mostly freshens a drain. Here is what each does, what it cannot do, and which products should never meet.

What works and what only feels like it works

Home remedies are worth trying on a slow sink or tub, and they cost almost nothing. They are not worth repeating five times in a row. If a remedy has not helped after two honest tries, the clog is farther down than you can reach.

Boiling water: useful, with two cautions

Pouring a kettle of hot water down a kitchen drain can loosen grease that has just started to build up. Pour slowly and in two or three rounds, giving each a few moments to work. Do not expect it to move hair or a wad of paper.

Never pour boiling water into a toilet, because sudden heat can crack porcelain. Be careful with plastic pipes and old joints too, since very hot water can soften them. Use hot tap water first, and keep boiling water for metal pipes you are sure of.

Baking soda and vinegar: a freshener, not a plunger

The foaming reaction looks impressive, but it is mostly carbon dioxide and water, so it does little against a solid blockage. It can lift light film and odor. A half cup of baking soda followed by a half cup of vinegar, left for fifteen minutes and flushed with hot tap water, is as far as it should go.

Plunger and hand snake, step by step

Clearing a single fixture

  1. 1

    Block the overflow

    Cover the overflow hole on a sink or tub with a wet rag so the pressure goes down the drain.

  2. 2

    Seal and plunge

    Cover the drain with water, seat the plunger flat and push and pull steadily for about twenty seconds.

  3. 3

    Check the flow

    If the water drops quickly, run hot water for a minute to carry the rest away.

  4. 4

    Try a hand snake

    Feed the cable in, turn the handle to hook the clog, pull it out and discard it in a bag.

  5. 5

    Stop if it fights you

    If the cable stops hard or comes out clean and the drain is still slow, the clog is deeper and the tool will not reach it.

What never to mix

People reach for everything under the sink when a drain will not clear, and that is when accidents happen. Some combinations release gas that can hurt your lungs in a closed room.

  • Bleach and ammonia, which makes toxic chloramine gas.
  • Bleach and vinegar, which makes chlorine gas.
  • Any chemical drain cleaner with another cleaner, or with a plunger working on the same clog.
  • Two different drain products on the same day, because the first may still be sitting in the pipe.

If a mixing accident happens, leave the room, open windows from the outside if you can, and call 911 if anyone has trouble breathing. Do not try to clean up the spill until the air has cleared.

Why store-bought chemical cleaners are a poor bet

Caustic drain products generate heat and can sit in a pipe that is fully blocked, waiting for someone to put a hand or a snake in. That is a burn risk for you and for the plumber who comes next. They also do little against hair and solid objects, and standing in old pipes they can eat at joints and fittings.

If you have already used one, tell the plumber before they open the trap. It lets them wear the right gloves and eye protection, and it is much better than a surprise. Our drain cleaning technicians ask that question first.

Local note

When the clog may be the tide, not your pipe

New Jersey figures cited in news coverage say water can rise through Atlantic City storm sewers during full-moon high tides and storms, and Rutgers projects 17 to 75 days per year of high-tide flooding by 2030. A drain that slows only at high tide is giving you information.

Check the tide before you start plunging or snaking. If every drain slows at once and it follows the tide, stop adding water and chemicals, and see our guidance on sewer line cleaning for storm-night main-line backups.

When a home remedy is a sign of a bigger problem

A home remedy that works once, then needs repeating every week, means something is catching in the same spot. The remedy is treating the symptom. These signs call for a plumber instead of another round of baking soda.

  • More than one fixture is slow or gurgling at the same time.
  • A toilet bubbles when you run the washing machine or a sink.
  • Water backs up in a tub or floor drain when you flush.
  • A sewage smell that comes and goes in the lowest room.
  • The clog returns within days of clearing.
  • Standing water in a basement floor drain or a cleanout.

Those point to the main line, where a hand snake cannot do the job and roots or a collapsed section may be involved. We use cables and cameras, and for grease or scale a hydro jetting pass.
