Can You Repair a Cracked Toilet Bowl in Atlantic City, NJ?

Sealants and epoxy can slow a hairline crack, but they are a short-term patch. A crack in the bowl, trap or tank wall can fail without warning and flood a bathroom. If water is seeping now, shut off the supply and plan a replacement.

Start here: is it leaking right now?

A crack that weeps water onto the floor is an active leak. Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise, flush once to empty the tank, and soak up standing water. Do not use the toilet again until a plumber has looked at it.

If the crack is hairline and dry, you have time to decide. The answer is almost always replacement, because porcelain does not heal. Our toilet repair team can tell you whether a part or the whole fixture is the right fix.

Where the crack is changes the answer

Tank crack

A crack in the tank wall is a leak waiting to grow. Tanks hold the most water, so failure can be sudden.

Bowl crack above the waterline

It may stay dry, but it can spread. A sealant patch is temporary at best.

Bowl crack below the waterline

Water will find it. Replacement is the realistic answer.

Base or flange crack

The bowl may rock or leak at the floor. A broken flange also needs repair.

Chip or surface craze

Cosmetic only if it does not go through the glaze. Watch it, but it often needs no action.

What sealants and epoxy actually do

Waterproof epoxy and porcelain repair kits can fill a small crack and hold for a while. They bond to the surface, but the toilet flexes with every flush and every person who sits on it. Over time the bond breaks, and the crack may open wider.

If you try a patch, use it as a bridge while you shop for a new toilet, not as a repair. Clean and dry the crack, apply the product as directed and keep an eye on the floor.

Do not use a patch on a crack that sits under the seat or at a bolt hole. Those spots carry load, and a patched crack there can give way when someone sits down. If the crack goes through the glaze and you can feel water behind it, plan to replace the fixture rather than relying on a bond that depends on a clean, dry surface.

Safe steps when you find a crack

  1. Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet and flush to empty the tank.
  2. Sponge out the remaining water in the bowl and tank so a cracked part is not holding weight.
  3. Wipe the crack dry and take a photo with a coin or ruler for scale.
  4. Check the floor around the base with a dry paper towel for dampness that suggests a slow leak.
  5. Keep children away from the fixture, since a cracked bowl can break under a person's weight.

Sharp porcelain edges can cut. Wear gloves if you handle broken pieces, and set them aside for the plumber.

When replacement is the right call

Replace the toilet when the bowl or trap is cracked, when the tank has a crack through the wall, or when the fixture is old and has needed several repairs. Replacement also lets you fix related problems, such as a rusted flange bolt, a failing shutoff valve or a rotted subfloor.

A tank crack on an otherwise sound bowl may be a candidate for a new tank, but matching parts to old models can be hard. Many owners find a full replacement cleaner. If it is a rental or a unit you manage, delaying risks water damage to the unit below.

Other reasons a toilet seems cracked

Hard water stains, glaze crazing and hairline scratches can look like cracks. Run a fingernail across it. A true crack catches the nail, and water seeps into it. If you cannot tell, a plumber can look quickly. For a toilet that clogs and overflows, drain cleaning may be the more pressing fix.
